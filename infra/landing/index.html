Crypto never closes, so the testing never stops.

Crypto AI scans three crypto futures markets every 60 seconds, runs 12 strategies against them, and paper-trades every signal with ₹1,00,000 of virtual capital under hard risk limits. A strategy has to hold up there, on the live market, before real money would ever be involved.

Paper trading only. Live trading is a deliberately gated last phase, and it isn't built.

The loop, stage by stage

The same strategy functions drive the backtester and the live scanner, so what was tested is exactly what runs.

  1. Scan

    Every 60 seconds, fresh Binance candles for each promoted strategy, coin and timeframe. Free public data; no API key.

  2. Signal

    A strategy that fires produces an entry, a stop, a target, a reward-to-risk ratio and a confidence, pushed live to the dashboard and, if configured, to Telegram or Discord.

  3. Filters

    Optional: a meta-label model scores the signal's chance of winning and drops it below 0.55. It won't train on fewer than 100 labelled samples.

  4. Risk gate

    Size from 1% risk, leverage within its cap, at most 5 open positions, and no new trades once the day's loss reaches 5%. All in one place.

  5. Paper broker

    Opens the position with fees, manages a trailing stop, accrues funding, and closes at the stop, the target or by hand.

  6. Record

    Every outcome is labelled and stored, with the market regime it happened in, for the review that follows.

  7. Review, then a person decides

    The adaptive agent reads the record and proposes bounded changes. Nothing is applied until it's approved, and anything applied can be reverted.

Risk is enforced in one place

Built to avoid fooling itself

A single good backtest window is easy to find and means little. Before a strategy is promoted to the live loop, it can be put through all of this.

Real costs in backtests

Fees, slippage on every fill, and the funding perpetual futures charge for holding a position.

Parameter sweep

A grid search with a heatmap, then a ±10% nudge to the best settings: if the edge collapses, it was fragile.

Out-of-sample split

Tuned on one stretch of history, judged on another it has never seen.

Walk-forward

Re-tuned and re-tested window after window, the way it would actually be used.

Monte Carlo

The trade order reshuffled 1,000 times to see how bad the drawdowns could plausibly get.

Buy-and-hold benchmark

Every result is shown next to simply holding the coin.

Results that look too good are refused, not promoted

  • A win rate above 85%
  • A profit factor above 5
  • Almost no drawdown across a real number of trades
  • Fewer than 15 trades, or an edge that doesn't hold out of sample

An agent that proposes, a person who decides

The adaptive layer reads how each strategy has done, in which market regime, and writes proposals. The proposals are computed from the record by rules; the language model only explains them in plain English. It never trades and never applies anything by itself.

What it can propose

  • disable_strategypause a strategy
  • enable_strategybring one back
  • set_size_multiplierclamped to 0.25–2×
  • set_regime_multipliersize by market regime
  • set_meta_enabledswitch the filter on or off
  • set_meta_thresholdtune the filter
  • update_paramsfrom a walk-forward run

How it's kept on a short lead

  • Every lever is small, clamped and reversible, so an approved proposal can never take the system past its hard risk caps.
  • Market regimes (trending up, trending down, ranging, high volatility) come from transparent rules on ADX, EMA slope and ATR, not a model that needs training data.
  • Outcomes are labelled with the triple-barrier method: target hit, stop hit, or time ran out.
  • Paper only. The agent's changes affect the paper account and nothing else.

How it's built, and its limits

  • scikit-learn

Everything runs on free data: Binance public market data, CoinGecko, the Fear & Greed index and news RSS. No paid API, no card on file. 123 automated tests.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Paper trading only. A live broker would plug into the same interface, but that phase is not built and won't be wired without a separate decision.
  • Live results are expected to trail backtests; that gap is what the paper phase exists to measure.
  • The AI commentary needs free-tier API keys and simply switches off without them.
  • Not investment advice. Leveraged futures are high-risk.
